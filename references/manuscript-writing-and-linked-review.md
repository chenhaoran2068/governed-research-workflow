# Manuscript Writing And Linked Review

This is generic guidance for writing and reviewing a human-governed manuscript.
It is not a journal instruction, reporting guideline, protocol, result review,
or submission decision. Apply a current target-journal instruction and an
applicable reporting guideline separately. Neither this guidance nor a
completed checklist establishes that any manuscript is scientifically correct,
complete, compliant, or ready to submit.

## Use Scientific Work To Set Narrative Weight

Section, subsection, and paragraph length should follow the scientific task,
evidence weight, and reader burden. Give consequential questions, design
choices, main results, and core interpretation enough space. Keep routine
background, standard procedures, supporting findings, and transitions tighter.
Do not make paragraphs mechanically equal in length, sentence count, or
rhetorical pattern.

Before drafting, identify the work the manuscript must do: establish the
problem and gap, define the design, report the main result, qualify it where
necessary, interpret it, and state limitations. A paragraph may be short when
it defines a term or reports one result. Split a paragraph when its scientific
job changes; do not expand it merely to match neighbouring paragraphs.

## Structure Results By Result Role

For an empirical original-research report, arrange Results by scientific logic:

1. study population and descriptive context;
2. the main result;
3. secondary findings; and
4. sensitivity, subgroup, or exploratory findings that qualify, test, or
   extend the main result.

Give the main result and the minimum explanation needed to read it the clearest
place. Detail follows result role and interpretive importance, not whether a
P value crosses a conventional threshold. Report prespecified primary and key
secondary outcomes even when they are null, imprecise, or inconvenient. A
sensitivity result belongs in the main text when it changes the credibility,
range, direction, or interpretation of the main result; otherwise it may be
summarised accurately and linked to its supplement.

Each Results paragraph should have one reporting job, such as reporting a main
estimate, establishing a necessary comparison, reporting a prespecified
secondary outcome, or presenting a robustness or exploratory check. Results
reports what the data show. Causal explanation, mechanism, literature
comparison, and broad implications belong in Discussion unless the selected
reporting profile requires another documented arrangement.

## Put Information Where A Reader Needs It

Place reproducibility-critical construction detail in Methods or Supplementary
Methods. Put realised counts, denominators, estimates, and missingness in
Results and the linked display. Put units, summary-statistic form,
abbreviations, local denominators, scale direction, and display-specific
exceptions in the table or figure that needs them.

Do not use a display note as the only home for an operational definition that a
reader needs to reproduce the work. Do not repeat the full Methods definition
in every Results paragraph. A supplement can hold detail, but it cannot hide a
material main-text result, replace a needed display explanation, or make a
reader guess the denominator for a reported estimate.

## State Limitations Clearly And Once Where Possible

Keep study-wide limitations together in a coherent, easy-to-find Discussion
paragraph or subsection. State the limitation, why it matters, and how it may
affect interpretation or generalisability. Keep a qualification next to a
specific result only when it is necessary for that result to be read correctly,
for example because the analysis is post hoc, restricted, sparse, or tied to a
defined time window.

Do not end every paragraph with a generic defensive sentence. Do not use
caution as a reason to omit the supported scientific point. This guidance does
not permit hiding bias, imprecision, missingness, multiplicity, or another
material constraint. Check that the conclusion and abstract retain every
qualification that materially changes their scope.

## Describe Cohort Eligibility In Two Layers

For a multi-condition cohort or routinely collected-data study, first describe
complete, non-overlapping inclusion and exclusion logic. Then describe how the
data implementation applies it: selection unit, case identification, time
anchor or window, duplicate handling, index-record choice, ordering or tie
rules, and material missingness handling.

State whether the unit is a person, admission, visit, ICU stay, episode,
record, or prediction time point. List joint inclusion conditions, and list
each independent exclusion once. Do not present an implementation detail as an
exclusion criterion or duplicate every inclusion condition as an inverse
exclusion. The selected study design and current reporting requirements decide
whether this pattern applies.

## Treat A Cohort Flow As Reconciled Evidence

When selection materially affects interpretation, the flow display must use the
same unit, rules, and counts as Methods, Results, code outputs, and relevant
table notes. Show distinct populations when denominators differ, such as
candidate, eligible, analysable, follow-up-complete, or outcome-specific
risk-set populations.

Each displayed step should reflect the order actually applied. Record incoming,
branch or exclusion, and outgoing counts. Do not show sequential subtraction
for overlapping exclusion reasons unless the overlap is labelled. Preserve an
editable source and inspect the rendered output for readable text, arrows,
counts, alignment, clipping, and page placement. A simple observational study
may not require a flow diagram; a more complex design may need another display
or multiple panels.

## Review Every Material Claim With Its Evidence

Review a material claim together with its nearby table, figure, supplement, or
source citation. Identify whether the claim is an estimate, direction,
comparison, uncertainty statement, interpretation, or limitation. Check what
the cited material supports and what it does not support. Track source-only,
numerical, display, and mixed changes separately.

When a change affects a display, citation, number, interpretation, or
translation, re-review directly affected text and dependent materials. Rebuild
and inspect the affected rendered output. A visual check complements, but does
not replace, evidence, statistical, or source review.

## Reconcile The Candidate Before A Human Decision

Before a candidate enters joint review or submission-readiness assessment,
identify the current source manuscript, editable displays, rendered package,
result package, QA record, and journal overlay if any. Reconcile the title,
abstract, objective, population, flow, main estimates, displays, references,
declarations, and supplements.

Use the Results-first work sequence and the selected joint-review profile. A
narrow revision needs review of its direct dependency chain, not automatic
re-review of unrelated pages. Keep unresolved author, ethics, access, funding,
conflict, journal, or approval facts visible as unresolved. Generated source
or rendered output is a candidate until the relevant result authority,
accountable-human scientific decision, and submission authority are present.
