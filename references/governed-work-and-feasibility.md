# Governed Work And Feasibility

## Human Control

Human-governed interactive work is the default. The user remains responsible
for research decisions, protocol choices, source access, scientific claims,
compliance facts, and release decisions.

Bounded autonomous execution is possible only after a written authorization
defines task scope, permitted inputs and outputs, exclusions, evidence,
stopping conditions, feedback route, approver, and review point. It never
authorizes unrestricted autonomy.

## Feasibility First

Before committing to a full study, identify:

- a focused question and intended contribution;
- source availability, access rights, and restrictions;
- population or unit of analysis;
- exposure, intervention, predictor, outcome, and timing definitions as
  relevant;
- the highest-risk analytic or data-construction step;
- the smallest critical-path test that can show whether the study is viable;
- the skills, approvals, compute, and time required;
- foreseeable stop, reframe, or escalation conditions.

Use assets/feasibility-brief.template.md. A feasibility result is evidence for
the next decision, not proof that a study is publishable.

## Authority And Data Boundaries

Identify the authoritative record for each purpose: protocol, source data,
analysis code, result, manuscript source, review rendering, or submission
package. Do not replace a locked or approved record by rerunning, reformatting,
or copying it without an accountable decision.

Do not request, copy, expose, or publish restricted, patient-derived, or
credentialed material through this skill. Record an authorized pointer or
access condition when that is sufficient.

## Consequential Stops

Ask for accountable evidence and approval before changing a protocol or
analysis plan, accepting a result, making a final claim, asserting compliance
facts, or releasing or submitting material.
