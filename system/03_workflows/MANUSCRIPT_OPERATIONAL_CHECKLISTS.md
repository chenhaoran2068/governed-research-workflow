# Manuscript Operational Checklists

## Purpose

This guidance helps a human-reviewed manuscript task make requirements,
evidence, uncertainty, and decision points visible. It is optional guidance
for a caller-named task; it does not write a manuscript, determine its content,
or make it ready for submission.

For a caller who asks how to organize a Results-first drafting and review
sequence, use `../../references/manuscript-and-submission-control.md` together
with this checklist. That reference explains a work order; it does not replace
this checklist, write content, or establish any manuscript fact.

For an explicitly selected default joint-review profile, the Results-first
sequence can be recorded as R4 through R10 in
`../../references/joint-review-profiles-and-dependency-order.md`. That record
tracks declared package order and reopen metadata only; it does not replace
the section, display, claim, citation, or human-review checks below.

## Requirement Uncertainty

Before treating a manuscript-facing artifact as ready for review, identify the
known requirement sources, unresolved requirements, relevant result and
evidence references, and the accountable human who must decide the next
consequential step. A template, draft, figure, or completed checklist item is
not evidence that a requirement is current, applicable, or satisfied.

When a requirement is unknown, conflicting, or changed, keep that uncertainty
visible and stop the affected claim, declaration, or package decision until a
human reviews an appropriate current source.

## Section, Display, And Paragraph Boundaries

For each section, display, or paragraph, keep its role visible:

- distinguish background statements from task findings;
- describe methods from approved task materials rather than infer them from
  outputs;
- keep results traceable to their stated result source without hand-adjusting
  values;
- distinguish supported interpretation, limitations, and hypotheses; and
- give each paragraph one clear evidence or reasoning role.

Tables, figures, and supplements need a stated purpose, title, notes,
abbreviation handling, and a traceable source or visible gap. A display is not
proof of a claim by itself.

## Claim, Evidence, And Citation Visibility

For a consequential claim, make the claimed basis and limits inspectable:

```text
claim
  -> supplied result or source evidence
  -> citation when an external statement is made
  -> limitation, uncertainty, or non-claim boundary
```

Do not invent a citation, treat a citation as a substitute for task evidence,
or describe a source as supporting a conclusion that has not been checked.
Keep exploratory, contradictory, negative, or uncertain material visible when
it affects how a claim should be read.

## Revision And Response Traceability

For a review comment or other requested revision, identify the trigger and
distinguish acknowledgement, explanation, change, and justified non-change.
A stated change should point to the revised artifact. A change that affects a
method, result, claim, source, declaration, or route needs the applicable human
review before a consequential transition.

Earlier reviewed or submitted material remains a historical version; a later
revision must not silently replace its identity.

## Declarations And AI-Use Facts

Draft declarations only from confirmed task-owned or externally verified facts.
Keep unknown facts as explicit follow-up items. Do not use a generic statement
to claim authorship, contribution, ethics, consent, funding, conflict, access,
or AI-use facts that have not been confirmed by the accountable human.

## Human Review Boundary

Before a human considers a named submission-facing package, ensure that open
requirements, artifact relationships, key claim bases, declarations, package
identity, and required review are visible. Passing this guidance does not prove
scientific validity, current external compliance, permission, authorship,
access, or submission readiness. Those are separate human decisions.
